Corvator

A Companion App to Happie, for Dividing Chores in Large Houses

Overview

An add on to Happie, for the part of a shared house that meal planning does not cover. Corvator makes skipped chores visible and turns them into a fair queue of penalty duties, so any large house can divide the work without argument. Built with Expo, React Native and TypeScript on a local SQLite database, so it works without an account or a connection.

The Problem

Same house of sixteen that Happie came out of. Cooking together was one problem; cleaning up after it was another. The dishes are not really a cleaning problem, they are an accounting problem: nobody remembers who left the pan on the stove three weeks ago, so nothing ever follows from it. Without a record, the only enforcement left is somebody nagging, and that cost always falls on the same person.

A rota on the fridge does not fix it either. It says whose turn it is, but not what happens when the turn is skipped.

The Solution

Corvator keeps a tally. Leave your mess and you get a mark. The first two are free, because everyone has a bad week; every mark after that becomes a penalty chore in a first-in-first-out queue. Each Sunday the app pulls four names off that queue and assigns the work. At the end of the month, remaining marks convert and the count resets.

The design choice that mattered was the free allowance. Punishing the first offence makes the app feel hostile and people stop opening it; forgiving two makes the third feel earned. Making the cost legible did what asking politely never did.

Under the hood it is a local-first app: versioned SQL migrations, one repository per table, and no server to keep running. A house should not need an account to decide who does the dishes.
