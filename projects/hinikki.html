HiNikki

A Voice Companion for Older Adults Living With Dementia

Overview

A voice companion for older adults living with dementia, and a family app that keeps its world accurate. The older adult gets one large button to talk to Nikki. The family gets a dashboard: schedule, reminders, safe places, emergency contacts, and a review queue of everything Nikki believes it has learned. On TestFlight and in active development. I lead the project and the small team building it.

The Problem

Someone living with dementia asks the same questions many times a day. What day is it. Is my daughter coming. Did I take my pills. A voice assistant can answer all of those, but a generic one answers generically, and gets it wrong in exactly the ways that matter, because it has no idea who your daughter is or whether she is actually coming.

Meanwhile the family carries the whole picture in their heads and in group chats, with no way to put it where it would help.

The Approach

Nikki's intelligence is deliberately not in our codebase. The conversation loop, meaning speech recognition, the language model, speech synthesis and turn taking, runs inside a specialist voice platform. What we build is the brain around it: everything that turns a generic voice model into this person's companion, and everything that turns a conversation into data a family can safely act on.

Before a call starts, the app assembles what Nikki should know right now: the person's profile, who is in their life, today's schedule, the weather where they actually are, and recent conversation summaries.

Nothing the AI Says Becomes a Fact

This is the part I would defend in a design review. A language model talking with a confused person is a data integrity problem wearing a friendly voice. If Nikki mishears "my son visited" and that quietly enters the family record, the record is now worse than useless. It is confidently wrong, and nobody knows.

So the boundary is drawn twice. Nothing in the transcript is ever stored as a fact; the agent has to call an explicit tool. And a session with the older adult cannot write to the family record at all, only propose. A family member reviews and approves. The elder cannot approve their own proposal and cannot forge an audit trail.

That boundary is enforced in two independent layers, in the tool layer and in the database security policy, because either one alone is a single point of failure. The failure you are defending against is not a hacker. It is a hallucination plus a person who cannot reliably tell you it was wrong.

Privacy

Voice transcripts, photos, schedules and precise location can constitute special category health data under the GDPR, regardless of how few users you have. A data protection impact assessment, a granular consent flow, a legal basis mapping per purpose and processor agreements are scoped and underway ahead of wider testing.
